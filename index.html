Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & Indicator Sampling Verification (Report Page 2)
Statistical Rigor

Page 2 of the report highlights the Central Limit Theorem: as urban indicator surveys are averaged across multiple municipal wards and sensor nodes, the standardized sample mean converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution: $$\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i, \quad Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$$ This mathematically justifies using composite indicators even when localized disaster damages follow highly skewed or bimodal distributions.

Full Mathematical Modeling Framework
Report Architecture

1. Indicator Normalization

Since resilience indicators use heterogeneous units (percentages, monetary loss, emergency minutes), min-max transformation scales every parameter to a bounded dimensionless domain \(z_{ij} \in [0, 1]\):

$$z_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - x_j^{\min}}{x_j^{\max} - x_j^{\min}} \quad \text{(Positive)}, \qquad z_{ij} = \frac{x_j^{\max} - x_{ij}}{x_j^{\max} - x_j^{\min}} \quad \text{(Negative)}$$

2. Baseline Capacity Aggregation

The four fundamental pillars—Infrastructure (\(P_1\)), Governance (\(P_2\)), Economy (\(P_3\)), and Social-Ecological Buffers (\(P_4\))—are aggregated using normalized weights:

$$C_i = \sum_{j=1}^m w_j z_{ij}, \quad \text{where } \sum_{j=1}^m w_j = 1, \quad C_i \in [0, 1]$$

3. Exponential Hazard Adjustment

A city's intrinsic capacity is discounted by an exponential penalty reflecting hazard exposure frequency and intensity:

$$NDRS_i = C_i \cdot e^{-\gamma H_i}, \quad \frac{\partial NDRS_i}{\partial H_i} = -\gamma C_i e^{-\gamma H_i} < 0$$

4. Time-Dependent Recovery Dynamics

Post-disaster operational restoration follows a concave exponential return function, whose definite integral provides the mean restored capacity:

$$Q_i(t) = 1 - L_{0,i} e^{-k_{r,i}(t - t_0)}, \qquad R_i(T) = \frac{1}{T}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T} Q_i(t) dt = 1 - \frac{L_{0,i}}{k_{r,i} T}\left(1 - e^{-k_{r,i} T}\right)$$
